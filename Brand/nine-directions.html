Sakokhule Trading (Pty) Ltd · nine marks

Three ways to be a logo.

Round two, properly split by type: wordmarks (type only, no icon — the safest, most credential-forward option for a bid pack), dynamic marks (an abstract symbol, no literal machine in it, built to work alone on a hard hat or a gate), and combination marks (icon and wordmark locked together, the way the first round was built). Nine total, three of each. Every lockup below carries the full registered name — Sakokhule Trading (Pty) Ltd — the way the current logo and every tender document already does.

Founded 2016 CIDB 5CE · 3GB Base Malelane, Mpumalanga Fleet grader · backhoe loader · tipper · water tanker Record R31.5m+ completed, since 2016

Still the one being replaced: generic growth clip art, blue and red, in a circle. Its two colours are the only thing carried forward on purpose — the site's clay-blue and terracotta were sampled directly off it, so every mark below stays inside that family and drops into the live site without a repaint.

Dynamic marks

An abstract symbol, no literal machine drawn — built to stand alone on a hard hat, a gate or a fleet decal at 20mm.

04
Haul line

Two moves, one company.

A double chevron — the plainest abstract way to draw forward motion. Two marks because there are two disciplines on the letterhead, earth moving and plant hire, moving the same direction at a slight offset rather than overlapping into one.

05
Cut layers

Four cuts, deepening.

Four parallel bars at a working angle, each one thicker and lower than the last — a plain abstraction of ground being cut through in layers, which is literally what regravelling and road-layer work is. No survey ticks, no baseline — purely the shape, for where the site version (Contour, round one) would be too fine a line to print small.

Rising cut

One wedge, two colours.

A single rising cut-face, steel-blue running into terracotta along the same diagonal — the old logo's two colours fused into one shape instead of split across a chart and an arrow. The most reducible of the nine: it survives being stamped one colour, embossed, or shrunk to a favicon.
